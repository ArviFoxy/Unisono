# The theorems

All 1795 theorems indexed below live in `lean/Unisono` and are checked in
`lean/Unisono/Audit.lean`, which pins the axioms each proof depends on via
`#guard_msgs` and `#print axioms`. Every theorem depends solely on Lean's three
standard classical axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). The
build fails if any proof contains `sorry` or introduces an unexpected axiom.

The proofs fall into three groups:

1. **Formal specification, models, topologies, and fundamental limits**
   (`Unisono.Spec.*`, plus the abstract reference solution
   `Unisono.Solutions.FixedDelay`): definitions of the problem, the six
   approved world models, communication topologies, and impossibility and
   separation theorems in the official framework (`docs/specification.tex`).
2. **Supplementary analytical results** (`Unisono.Model.*`,
   `Unisono.Analysis.*`): component-level and centralized/oracle analyses in
   simplified settings (real numbers, fixed filtrations, per-cycle quadratic
   approximations, continuous-time pause formulas). Per `PRINCIPLES.md`, these
   serve as analytical references and lower-bound benchmarks, never as
   replacements for end-to-end proofs in `Unisono.Spec`.
3. **Production core code** (`Unisono.*` and `Unisono.Solutions.Core`): proofs
   directly about the Aeneas-generated Lean translation of
   `crates/unisono-core`. The generated translation files
   (`lean/UnisonoCore/{Types,Funs}.lean`) are regenerated on demand by
   `tools/extract.sh` (`just translate` / `just prove`), are not checked into
   git, and are cross-checked against Rust execution on concrete inputs by
   `just translation-check` (`crates/translation-check` and
   `lean/TranslationCheck.lean`).

"Returns for every input" below means totality: the function terminates without
integer overflow, out-of-bounds indexing, or panic for every state and every
argument, whether or not reachable in a run.

### 1.6 The controller: playback at a level that the sender announces (`Solutions.Controller`)

`Solutions.Controller.solution` sends the baseline's messages with further
fields and plays differently. The sender announces with every block the
level of the block's first frame, which is the latency that every speaker is
to hold for it, and the slope of the level; how it chooses the level is a
parameter (`LevelRule`). The sender estimates the capture time of each frame
on its clock as a line through the capture device's timestamps, each
receiving host estimates the sender's clock as a line in its own clock from
its clock exchanges, and each host estimates when each output sample leaves
as a line through its playback device's timestamps. The estimators are
parameters. A speaker plays, at each output sample, the position whose
capture time plus level is the sender's clock reading at which the sample
leaves. Each frame has the level announced with its own block: a host keeps
the header of every block that arrived and uses, for an output sample, the
header of a block whose own frames contain the position that the header says
is due (`Header.Covers`, `dueOf`), so that every host changes the level at
the same frame. When the clocks of the hosts and of the audio devices run at constant rates,
each its own, and the level is held at `L`, the position that is due at an
output sample is the one captured `L` divided by the rate of the sender's
clock, plus the asymmetry of the speaker's host, before the sample leaves.
When links and hosts pause, from some output sample on each output sample
plays that position if the message of the capture block it needs took less
than the sample's margin, and plays nothing of the source otherwise
(`speaker_plays`, `plays_eq_of_margin`, `plays_under_pauses`). The margin of
an output sample is the time from the handover of the capture block it needs
to the request of its own block. It lies between two numbers that do not
depend on the sample, and both rise by one second for each second of latency
(`margin_mem`). Without pauses every block is in time, and the speaker plays
every frame at that latency (`playsAtLatencies`). When the level changes
and each block starts at the level at which the block before it ended,
the same holds with the level of each position in place of `L`: an output
sample plays nothing of the source or the one position that was captured,
before the sample leaves, the latency of its own level, and it plays that
position exactly when the capture block that the position needs arrived
before the request (`speaker_plays_of_levels`). The held level is the case
of a constant announcement. One rule with such announcements is
`steppedLevel`: the sender keeps a level that it aims at, moves the aim one
step against the slope of the cost of each need that a clock query reports,
and at each handover announces a slope that moves the level toward the aim
by at most `slopeMax` per frame (`announced_steppedLevel_succ`,
`speaker_plays_of_steppedLevel`). What a host reports in a clock query is a
parameter of the level rule. The need of a capture block at a speaker is
a level at which the block is in time: when the level of a position that
is due is at least the need of the block that the position needs, that
block arrives before the request (`arrival_lt_asked_of_needOf_le`). A host
can compute it from its own estimates and the block's header
(`measuredNeed`, `needOfBlock_eq`). Nothing yet says which needs reach the
sender or what the rule achieves.

An output sample is late when the message of the capture block it needs
takes at least the sample's margin, and the late rate of a level is the
long-run number of late output samples per second, averaged over the
speakers. The cost of a held level is at most the cost of the speakers'
latencies in synchronization, latency and tracking, plus a multiple of the
late rate (`cost_le_of_heldLevel`). The first part rises with the level and
the late rate falls with it.

These theorems hold in one outcome, and `plays_under_pauses` assumes of the
outcome that two clock exchanges of each receiving host escape every pause.
In every random world of a member with steady clocks this holds in almost
every outcome (`ae_twoUndelayed`), and with it the bound on the cost by the
late rate of the outcome (`ae_cost_le_heldLevel`). The long-run fraction of
capture blocks whose message to a speaker's host takes longer than the
speaker's least margin is, in almost every outcome, a probability of the
member (`ae_tendsto_slowBlocks`), and the late rate is at most the mean over
the speakers of that probability times the sum of the speaker's output
samples per second and the capture blocks per second (`ae_lateRate_le`). So
the expected cost of the controller at a held level is at most the mean of
an explicit function of what is drawn, and that mean is finite
(`expectedCost_le_heldLevelCost`, `lintegral_heldLevelCost_lt_top`). The
controller does not keep the speed bound of the solutions that it is
compared with (`Solution.WithinStep`). With a limiter it keeps the bound in
every world (`limited_withinStep`), and its expected cost at a held level

### 1.7 Optimality in expected cost (`Spec.Optimality`)

`OptimalWithin` defines what it means for a solution to be optimal within a
margin: its expected cost exceeds that of no compared solution by more than
the margin, in every random world of every member of a family of laws. The
solutions compared at a member depend on that member alone. A margin of `⊤`
at a member says nothing about that member. A statement about
`GenerativeWorld` is always about every member
(`GenerativeWorld.OptimalWithin`), so a statement that covers only some
members shows the margin `⊤` for the others.
No solution is proved optimal. One bound of this form is proved
(`Solutions.FixedDelay.within_starMargin`): in the noiseless members of
`GenerativeWorld`, which are the members with every disturbance switched
off, the baseline's expected cost exceeds that of no compared solution
through the sender by more than a known margin. A solution through the
sender (a star solution) is one whose hosts exchange messages only with the
sender. In every other member the margin of the statement is `⊤`, so it says
nothing there, and it does not compare solutions that use links between
receiving hosts. By `PRINCIPLES.md` this does not make the baseline
optimal.

Nothing here is about a solution of the specification yet: the controller does not use this rule. |

## 4. What is not proved (Open)

1. **No objective bounds for `Core.solution` in any model:**
   `Unisono.Solutions.Core` proves causality, communication-graph membership,
   exact Star degeneration, non-Zeno progress and request liveness under
   `World.WithinBounds`, within-block speed bounds unconditionally under
   `World.WithinBounds` (plus the conditional adjacent-block bound
   `boundary_step_safe`), and component-level exactness on ideal constant
   inputs. No theorem currently connects a run of `Core.solution` to any of the
   six `Unisono.Spec.Objectives` in any model (`IdealConstantWorld`,
   `World.WithinBounds`, `StationaryNetworkDisturbances`,
   `SecondOrderMoments`, `StochasticWorld`, or `GenerativeWorld`).
2. **Unused stochastic world hypotheses:** no theorem in the repository
   currently takes `SecondOrderMoments`, `StochasticWorld` or `GenerativeWorld`
   as a hypothesis;
   constructing a random strictly increasing `Clock` with nonzero `ClockNoise`
   also remains open.
3. **`WithinSpeed` and `ideal_zero_cost_objectives` boundaries:**
   - Unconditional `Solution.WithinSpeed` for `Core.solution` across all block
     boundaries (including silence restarts and arbitrary `jump` settings) is
     not yet proved; `boundary_step_safe` proves the non-restart, non-overflow,
     `jump ≥ 3` boundary condition separately from `withinBlock_speed_bound`.
   - `ideal_zero_cost_objectives` takes the steady-state unity-step trajectory
     as its hypothesis; wiring the full event-driven run of `Core.solution`
     from startup lock into that steady-state trajectory on
     `IdealConstantWorld.Symmetric` remains open.
4. **Integer-to-real estimator equivalence (`MinRtt`, `Kalman`, `Blue`):**
   - `MinRtt.lean` proves totality, exact single-exchange NTP arithmetic, ring
     size, variance floors, and deduplication, but not that `SlidingMinRtt`
     emits the exact minimum RTT across its window.
   - `Kalman.lean` proves totality, zero-innovation fixed-point preservation,
     and PSD preservation via explicit clamping in `enforce_psd`, but not
     equivalence or error bounds against the real-valued Kalman recursion.
   - `Blue.lean` proves totality, variance/count invariants, zero right-hand-side
     fixed point, and exact single-edge Star recovery
     (`solve_blue_single_edge_star`), but not finite-precision error bounds
     comparing integer `LDL^T` against real-valued
     `Analysis.Estimation.thomson_blue_least_variance` on multi-peer graphs.
5. **Other core gaps:** `PeerRelay` byte-level round-trip (totality and kind
   separation are proved; round-trip is covered by Rust unit tests and
   `translation-check`), lower-hull fit existence in `Relation`, cycle-step
   rounding error, and explicit loop-round counts in `Rounds.lean` for
   network-thread loops (`MinRtt`, `Kalman`, `Blue`, `Sync`, which are proved
   total in their own modules).
6. **Code outside `crates/unisono-core` (tested, not proved):** the
   floating-point adaptive latency chooser (`crates/unisono-runtime/src/level.rs`,
   whose unlinked real-valued companion is `Model.Level`), retransmission
   tracking and operating-system socket, clock, and ring-buffer runtime
   (`crates/unisono-runtime`), digital signal processing (resampling, sample
   conversion, fades, and gap concealment in `crates/unisono-dsp`), audio
   backends (`unisono-pipewire`, `unisono-alsa`), metrics, the C library, and
   the command-line program are checked by unit tests, multi-seed true-time
   simulation (`crates/unisono-sim`), Loom concurrency interleavings,
   machine-code inspection (`crates/reachable`, `crates/cycle-cost`), and
   container tests against PipeWire and Advanced Linux Sound Architecture
   (ALSA).
7. **Optimality in expected cost under `GenerativeWorld`:** no solution is
   proved optimal. The one bound of that form compares the baseline with
   solutions through the sender only, and its margin is finite in noiseless
   members only (`Solutions.FixedDelay.within_starMargin`). No lower bound on expected cost is
   proved for solutions that use links between receiving hosts, and no lower
   bound on expected cost is proved in members with pauses or clock wander;
   an upper bound on the expected cost of the controller at a held level,
   in members with steady clocks, is in item 8. The compared star solutions
   are those that keep the speed bound: their position advances by one frame
   per output sample within a bound below one, at every step of continuous
   playback (`Solution.WithinStep`). The bound is on the ratio at which the
   resampler plays against its own device, which a solution chooses; a bound
   on the speed in true time (`Solution.WithinSpeed`) cannot be kept by any
   solution when the rates of the devices' clocks have laws without bounds; the margin is finite only where the weight of missed
   samples is large enough against the round-trip times
   (`GenerativeWorld.MissedWeightPays`). Without a bound on the speed no
   lower bound on the cost holds: a playback that moves forward ever more
   slowly, without reaching the next frame, costs nothing. Random worlds
   are constructed for every member whose clocks do not wander and whose
   audio devices report exact timestamps
   (`GenerativeWorld.exists_holds_of_steadyClocks`): its links and its hosts
   may pause, with any laws of the lengths, and the mean rates of its clocks
   may be spread. In every random world of a member, the paused times of
   the links and hosts have one law (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.map_paused`), and
   almost every outcome has periods of any length in which no link and no
   host has a backlog (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.ae_idle`). A random world with
   wander, or with errors of the
   timestamps, needs a stationary Gaussian process with continuous paths,
   which is not constructed in Lean. The expectation that a solution loses nothing in the long run by not
   being told the numbers of its member is not proved. The generative
   model is not shown to belong to the model classes above.
8. **The controller under the generative model:** `Solutions.Controller` is
   described output sample by output sample, and only in worlds whose clocks
   run at constant rates with exact timestamps. The cost of a held level is
   bounded in each outcome by the late rate of that outcome, and in almost
   every outcome of every random world of a member with steady clocks two
   clock exchanges of each receiving host escape every pause, as that bound
   needs (`Solutions.Controller.ae_twoUndelayed`). For messages sent at
   equally spaced fixed true times, the long-run fraction of late ones is,
   in almost every outcome of every random world, a probability of the
   member (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.ae_tendsto_lateFraction`), also when the
   times of sending depend on the drawn clocks
   (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.ae_lateFraction_scalars`). In a member with steady
   clocks this gives the long-run fraction of capture blocks that are slower
   than the least margin of a speaker
   (`Solutions.Controller.ae_tendsto_slowBlocks`), a bound on the late rate
   of the controller (`Solutions.Controller.ae_lateRate_le`), and a bound on
   its expected cost at a held level by the finite mean of an explicit
   function of what is drawn
   (`Solutions.Controller.expectedCost_le_heldLevelCost`,
   `lintegral_heldLevelCost_lt_top`). With a limiter the controller keeps
   the speed bound of the compared solutions in every world, and its
   expected cost at a held level has a bound of the same form
   (`Solutions.Controller.limited_withinStep`,
   `expectedCost_le_limitedCost`). The bound on the late rate charges one
   output sample too many for each slow capture block. The probability that
   a message is late (`GenerativeWorld.lateProbability`) is a number of the
   member that is not evaluated: no formula or bound for it in terms of the
   rates and the lengths of the pauses is proved. For every solution through the sender, an output sample
   plays a position only if the frames it needs can have arrived
   (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.arrival_lt_of_plays`), and a stretch of output
   samples without new frames costs missed samples or discontinuities
   (`Spec.stall_le_of_le`). Together: while the message of a capture block
   is on its way, the output samples of a speaker cost missed samples or
   discontinuities, except for what the frames already held allow
   (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.stall_le`). Paused time of the link from the
   sender that begins in a short interval gives such a stretch, and until it
   can be noticed a solution plays as in the world without it
   (`GenerativeWorld.Builds.stall_le_of_sameExcept`); the frames that a
   speaker holds beyond the position it plays are paid for with latency
   (`GenerativeWorld.Builds.le_sum_add_loss`); with the weights of the
   latency objective, and summed over the first playback blocks of a
   speaker, the losses are paid for by missed samples
   (`GenerativeWorld.Builds.sum_blocks_le_latency`). These hold in one
   outcome of every member, also when clocks wander and timestamps have
   errors. In every random world of every member, what a link pauses
   within a period can be drawn again from an independent outcome, and so
   can all the pauses together (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.prior_resample`,
   `Prior.prior_resampleLinks`). These are not yet a lower bound on
   expected cost. Not proved: a choice of
   the level; a lower bound on the cost of other solutions under pauses;
   anything when clocks wander or timestamps have errors. In an outcome in
   which the capture device's clock runs at least twice as fast as the clock
   of some playback device, the bound on the cost charges every output
   sample; such outcomes have positive probability when the mean rates of
   the clocks are spread. For the controller with a limiter the bound
   charges every output sample in more outcomes: those in which the rate of
   the capture device's clock divided by the rate of the clock of some
   playback device is not within the speed bound of one. Its pace does not
   yet change smoothly,
   and it does not yet measure what frames need or choose the level; the
   regulator and the choice of the level exist only as side models
   (`Model.Regulation`, `Model.Level`) and in the production code. Random
   worlds with pauses exist (`GenerativeWorld.exists_holds_of_steadyClocks`),
   and in a member with steady clocks almost every outcome is a world of the
   kind that these theorems describe
   (`GenerativeWorld.Prior.ae_constantRates`).
