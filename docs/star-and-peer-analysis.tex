\begin{center}
{\LARGE\bfseries Playback control, clock estimation,\\[2pt]
and playout buffering for star and peer topologies\par}
\end{center}
\medskip

This report analyzes multi-speaker audio synchronization under two
communication topologies for the problem defined in \path{docs/specification.pdf}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Star topology} (\lean{Solution.IsStar}), where every network
  message either leaves or enters the sender (no receiver-to-receiver traffic).
\item \textbf{Peer topology} (\lean{Solution.UsesGraph} with
  receiver--receiver edges), where receiving hosts also exchange clock probes
  and playback-state messages with one another.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope and what is formally proved.}
To keep the boundary between formal proofs and modeling approximations clear:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{On the specification (\lean{Unisono.Spec}) and production core
  (\lean{Unisono.Solutions.Core}).} We prove the per-frame modal decomposition
  of \lean{World.syncCost} and \lean{World.trackingCost}, the per-cycle
  roughness scaling for linear speed ramps and boundary steps, a host-timeline
  shift lower bound on \lean{World.sync} and \lean{World.tracking}, and a
  separation theorem on two three-host worlds with ideal clocks and constant
  delays, whose sender links have one-way asymmetries $+A$ and $-A$
  (\lean{peer\_beats\_star\_asymmetry\_bound} in \path{Spec/Limits.lean}). In
  one of them, every star solution meeting the hypotheses of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:star-peer-sep} (including \lean{FixedDelay.solution} and the
  tight forwarding-only witness \lean{ForwardStar.solution}) has
  synchronization cost at least $A^2$, while a peer witness written for these
  worlds (\lean{PeerSync.solution}) has cost $0$ in both. We also compose the
  Aeneas-translated Rust production core (\path{crates/unisono-core}) into a
  \lean{Unisono.Spec.Solution} in \path{Solutions/Core.lean} (a Lean
  composition of the core functions, separate from \path{unisono-runtime}) and
  prove communication-graph membership, exact Star degeneration when no peers
  are configured, non-Zeno progress and block-request liveness under
  \lean{World.WithinBounds}, within-block playback-speed bounds under
  \lean{World.WithinBounds} (plus a conditional adjacent-block step bound when
  \lean{jump} $\ge 3$, no silence restart, and no \texttt{u64::MAX} overflow),
  and component-level exactness on noiseless constant inputs. Unconditional
  \lean{Solution.WithinSpeed} and all six \lean{Unisono.Spec.Objectives} for
  runs of \lean{Core.solution} remain open in every model.
\item \textbf{Per-cycle linear-quadratic control
  (\lean{Analysis.ModalControl}, \lean{Analysis.Spectral}).} These
  supplementary side-model theorems minimize an auxiliary per-cycle quadratic
  cost (\lean{multiCycleCost}) rather than the full nonlinear \lean{World}
  objectives. Multi-speaker minimization theorems assume one shared observation
  filtration and therefore act as \emph{centralized benchmarks} (lower bounds
  on \lean{multiCycleCost} for decentralized controllers). For independent star
  receivers, we prove minimization of \lean{multiCycleCost} only when
  cross-receiver position errors are pairwise uncorrelated; when the sender's
  clock wanders, we prove a common-plus-private cost identity and evaluate one
  specific decentralized filter-plus-regulator Lyapunov equation numerically.
\item \textbf{Linear graph estimation (\lean{Analysis.Estimation}) and
  standalone Campbell playout buffering (\lean{Analysis.Level}).} Clock-offset
  estimation theorems characterize linear unbiased estimators on graphs via
  Kirchhoff flows and Thomson's principle, including bias formulas under
  asymmetric links. Playout margin theorems analyze a standalone
  continuous-time Campbell pause model (not imported into
  \lean{Unisono.Spec.Objectives}).
\end{itemize}

Formal proofs reside in \path{Spec/Topologies.lean}, \path{Spec/Limits.lean},
\path{Solutions/Core.lean}, and the \path{Analysis/} directory under
\path{lean/Unisono/}, checked in \path{lean/Unisono/Audit.lean} (1146 theorems)
using only the standard Lean axioms. The second-order noise model is in

\section{Problem framing, modal decomposition, and weight conversion}
\label{sec:framing}

The formal specification (\path{docs/specification.pdf}) evaluates a solution
against six long-run objectives on a continuous-time event-driven world
(\lean{World}): synchronization ($c_S$), mean latency ($c_\Lambda$), latency
tracking ($c_\Theta$), playback roughness ($c_R$), missed samples ($c_M$), and
discontinuities ($c_D$). This section states the identities that hold on
\lean{World} itself and records the approximations and unit conversions linking
\lean{World} to the per-cycle linear-quadratic control problem of
Section~\ref{sec:control}.

\subsection{Modal decomposition of frame objectives}

For $R > 0$ speakers and a frame $n$ played by every speaker at true times
$T_1(n), \dots, T_R(n)$, let $\ell_r(n) = T_r(n) - \tau_{\mathrm{cap}}(n)$ be
speaker $r$'s latency relative to capture time $\tau_{\mathrm{cap}}(n)$.
Decompose $\ell(n) \in \R^R$ into its common mode (group mean) and differential
mode (zero-mean deviations):
\[
  \bar\ell = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R \ell_r,
  \qquad
  \delta_r = \ell_r - \bar\ell,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Var}(\ell) = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R \delta_r^2 .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Modal decomposition of \lean{World} frame objectives]
\label{thm:modal-spec}
In \path{lean/Unisono/Spec/Topologies.lean} (\lean{syncCost\_eq\_var},
\lean{trackingCost\_eq\_sync\_add\_mean}, \lean{weighted\_frame\_modal\_split},
and \lean{modal\_sq\_split}), for every frame $n$ played by all $R > 0$
speakers and every specification weight tuple $c$:
\begin{align*}
  \mathrm{syncCost}(n) &= \operatorname{Var}(\ell), \\
  \mathrm{trackingCost}(n) &= \operatorname{Var}(\ell)
    + \bigl(\bar\ell - \mathrm{target}(n)\bigr)^2, \\
  c_S\,\mathrm{syncCost}(n) + c_\Theta\,\mathrm{trackingCost}(n)
    &= (c_S + c_\Theta)\,\operatorname{Var}(\ell)
    + c_\Theta\,\bigl(\bar\ell - \mathrm{target}(n)\bigr)^2 .
\end{align*}
In addition, for any real vector $x \in \R^R$,
$\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R x_r^2 = \bar x^2 + \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R (x_r - \bar x)^2$.
\end{theorem}

The algebraic identity on $\R^R$ applies to per-cycle pace-change vectors
$\Delta u_k \in \R^R$ in the per-cycle control model of
Section~\ref{sec:control}. It does \emph{not} decompose the specification's own
per-sample \lean{World.roughness} without assuming equal-size cycles aligned in
true time across all speakers.

Theorem~\ref{thm:modal-spec} separates the two operating modes of the
specification: in lip-sync mode the mean latency has weight $c_\Theta$, and the
deviations from it have weight $c_S + c_\Theta$. In low-latency mode
($c_\Theta = 0$) the mean latency costs $c_\Lambda$ per second of latency, and
it causes missed samples when it falls below the network delay.

\subsection{Per-cycle roughness scaling and weight conversion}

The specification charges roughness per output sample:
\lean{World.roughness} sums $(s_{j+1} - s_j)^2 / (t_{j+1} - t_j)$ over each
speaker's output samples, where $s_j$ is the instantaneous playback speed. By
contrast, the per-cycle control problem in Section~\ref{sec:control} charges
squared pace change $\Delta u^2$ once per cycle in frames per cycle. Converting
specification weights $(c_S, c_\Theta, c_R)$ into per-cycle weights requires
the time-scaling factor proved in \path{lean/Unisono/Spec/Topologies.lean}.

\begin{theorem}[Per-cycle roughness and position-weight conversion]
\label{thm:weight-conv}
Let $\rho > 0$ be the frame rate, $\Delta t > 0$ the cycle length, and
$M = \rho\Delta t \in \mathbb{N}_+$ the number of output samples per cycle
(with uniform sample spacing $1/\rho$).
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\lean{World.roughness\_linear\_ramp}) If the playback speed changes
  linearly across the $M$ samples of a cycle to achieve a pace change of
  $\Delta u$ frames per cycle, the specification roughness accrued over that
  cycle is $\Delta u^2 / (\rho^2 \Delta t^3)$.
\item (\lean{World.roughness\_boundary\_step}) If the same pace change
  $\Delta u$ is applied as a single step at the cycle boundary, the
  specification roughness accrued over that cycle is
  $M \cdot \Delta u^2 / (\rho^2 \Delta t^3)$—that is, $\rho\Delta t$ times as
  much.
\item (\lean{cyclePositionWeight\_eq}, an algebraic identity on real numbers
  that mentions neither \lean{World} nor \lean{Weights}) Let the speed ramp
  linearly within a cycle. A position error of $e$ frames held for the cycle
  costs $c_{\mathrm{pos}}\cdot(e / \rho)^2\cdot\Delta t$. A pace change of
  $\Delta u$ frames per cycle costs $c_R\cdot\Delta u^2 / (\rho^2 \Delta t^3)$.
  The sum is $c_R / (\rho^2 \Delta t^3)$ times
  $\tilde c_{\mathrm{pos}}\cdot e^2 + \Delta u^2$, with
  \[
    \tilde c_{\mathrm{pos}} = \frac{c_{\mathrm{pos}}}{c_R}\,\Delta t^4 .
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

For $\Delta t = 10\,\text{ms}$, $\Delta t^4 = 10^{-8}\,\text{s}^4$, and a
boundary step at $\rho = 48\,\text{kHz}$ ($M = 480$) incurs $480$ times the
roughness of a linear ramp. Substituting specification weights $c_S, c_\Theta$
into a per-cycle gain formula without multiplying by $\Delta t^4 / c_R$ gives
the wrong gain.

\subsection{Assumptions separating the control model from the specification}

No theorem in this repository bounds the full nonlinear \lean{World} objective
of a concrete \lean{Solution} by the per-cycle cost of
Section~\ref{sec:control}. The control results in Section~\ref{sec:control}
rely on the following modeling assumptions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Aligned equal-duration cycles.} All $R$ speakers share the same
  cycle length $\Delta t$ and compare positions at a common cycle index $k$.
\item \emph{Linear speed ramps within cycles.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:weight-conv},
  per-cycle squared pace change $\Delta u_{r,k}^2$ matches
  \lean{World.roughness} up to $1/(\rho^2\Delta t^3)$ only when speed is ramped
  linearly across each cycle.
\item \emph{Interior speed regime and unconstrained continuity.} Minimization of
  \lean{multiCycleCost} by \lean{Regulation.law} holds without hard speed caps,
  continuity constraints, or data-availability constraints. Practical
  implementations clamp playback speed deviation from nominal rate $1$ to
  $[-s_{\max}, s_{\max}]$.
\item \emph{Omitted terms.} \lean{multiCycleCost} excludes linear mean latency
  ($c_\Lambda$), missed samples ($c_M$), and discontinuities ($c_D$); those
  enter separately through the playout margin model in Section~\ref{sec:level}.
\item \emph{Fixed observation filtration (no dual control).} Playback actions
  do not alter network delivery times, so each filtration $(\mathcal{F}_k)$ is
  independent of the playback policy.
\item \emph{Martingale one-step expected increments.} The target advance
  satisfies $\E[S_{k+2} - S_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] =
  \E[S_{k+1} - S_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k]$ almost surely. This holds for
  integrated white and random-walk frequency noise ($h_0$ and $h_{-2}$ terms),
  and fails under $1/f$ flicker frequency noise, varying block sizes, or changes
  in the target latency.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Per-cycle linear-quadratic playback control}
\label{sec:control}

Consider $R > 0$ speakers operating over equal-duration cycles
$k = 0, 1, \dots$. Speaker $r$ starts cycle $k$ at source position $p_{r,k}$,
reads $u_{r,k}$ frames during cycle $k$ ($p_{r,k+1} = p_{r,k} + u_{r,k}$), and
incurs pace change $\Delta u_{r,k} = u_{r,k} - u_{r,k-1}$. Let $S_{r,k}$ denote
the due source position at the start of cycle $k$ (the position captured $L$
seconds before the first output sample of cycle $k$, where $L$ is the target
latency), and define speaker $r$'s \emph{position error}
$e_{r,k} = S_{r,k} - p_{r,k}$. Normalized by roughness weight, the per-cycle
cost (\lean{multiCycleCost} in
\path{lean/Unisono/Analysis/ModalControl.lean}) is
\[
  \mathrm{cost}_k = \tilde c_S\,\operatorname{Var}_r(e_{r,k})
      + \frac{\tilde c_\Theta}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R e_{r,k}^2
      + \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R \Delta u_{r,k}^2 .
\]
For any gain $g \in (0, 1)$, define
$\mathrm{weight}(g) = g^4 / \bigl((2 - g)^2(1 - g)\bigr)$. For any positive
per-cycle weight $\tilde c > 0$, \lean{gainForWeight} $\tilde c$ denotes the
unique $g \in (0, 1)$ satisfying $\mathrm{weight}(g) = \tilde c$
(\lean{weight\_gainForWeight}, \lean{gainForWeight\_unique}). (Note: the
earlier helper \lean{Regulation.gainFor} takes the fourth root $\tilde c^{1/4}$,
satisfying $\mathrm{weight}(\mathrm{gainFor}(\tilde c^{1/4})) = \tilde c$;
passing $\tilde c$ directly to \lean{gainFor} would pass $\tilde c^4$ instead
of $\tilde c$.)

Given an observation filtration $(\mathcal{F}_k)$, let
$E_k = \E[S_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k] - p_k$ be the estimated position lag and
$v_k = u_{k-1} - \E[S_{k+1} - S_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k]$ the excess pace above
the expected source advance. The feedback control rule \lean{Regulation.law}
computes the pace change
\[
  \mathrm{law}(g, E_k, v_k) = \frac{g^2}{2 - g}\,E_k - g\,v_k,
\]
and $V_g(E_k, v_k) = \mathrm{value}(g, E_k, v_k)$ denotes the positive definite
quadratic value-to-go of \path{Unisono.Model.Regulation}.

\subsection{Single-mode separation and almost-sure uniqueness}

\begin{theorem}[Single-mode separation and uniqueness]
\label{thm:single-sep}
In \path{Analysis/ModalControl.lean} (\lean{single\_cost\_split},
\lean{single\_law\_minimizes}, \lean{single\_law\_unique\_ae}), assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item $(\mathcal{F}_k)$ is a filtration on a probability space $(\Omega, \Prob)$,
\item $S_k \in L^2(\Prob)$ satisfies
  $\E[S_{k+2} - S_{k+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_k] =
   \E[S_{k+1} - S_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k]$ almost surely for all $k$,
\item initial position $p_0$ and initial pace $u_{-1}$ are
  $\mathcal{F}_0$-known and square-integrable, and $g \in (0, 1)$.
\end{itemize}
Then for every horizon $n \in \mathbb{N}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $\mathcal{F}$-adapted policy $u$, the expected $n$-cycle cost
  plus terminal value-to-go splits into a policy-independent estimation floor
  (from $\operatorname{Var}(S_k \mid \mathcal{F}_k)$ and conditional-mean
  revisions) plus the control cost of the conditional-mean state.
\item Applying \lean{Regulation.law} with gain $g$ to the conditional-mean
  state (\lean{lawRead}) is $\mathcal{F}$-adapted and minimizes the expected
  $n$-cycle cost plus terminal value-to-go over all $\mathcal{F}$-adapted
  policies, for any noise distribution with finite second moments. (In
  practice, a Kalman filter computes exact conditional means only for a
  linear-Gaussian model with known noise parameters.)
\item Any $\mathcal{F}$-adapted policy achieving the minimum equals
  \lean{lawRead} almost surely at every cycle $k < n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Centralized shared-filtration benchmarks}

Suppose all $R$ speakers had access to one common filtration $(\mathcal{F}_k)$
containing every speaker's observations up to cycle $k$. In practice, peer
messages arrive with delay and packet loss, so no distributed peer protocol can
attain a common $\mathcal{F}_k$, and coordinating through the sender is a star.
The next two theorems are therefore \emph{centralized benchmarks}: they give
lower bounds on the per-cycle cost \lean{multiCycleCost} achievable by any
decentralized star or peer policy using less information.

\begin{theorem}[Centralized two-gain lip-sync benchmark and long-run bounds]
\label{thm:lipsync-opt}
In \path{ModalControl.lean} (\lean{multi\_lipsync\_minimizes},
\lean{multi\_lipsync\_unique\_ae}, \lean{multi\_lipsync\_cost\_rate},
\lean{multi\_lipsync\_law\_cost\_le}), assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item $R > 0$, $\tilde c_\Theta = \mathrm{weight}(g_{\mathrm{com}})$, and
  $\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta = \mathrm{weight}(g_{\mathrm{diff}})$ with
  $g_{\mathrm{com}}, g_{\mathrm{diff}} \in (0, 1)$,
\item all $R$ speakers share one filtration $(\mathcal{F}_k)$, with
  square-integrable targets $S_{r,k}$ satisfying the one-step martingale
  increment condition and $\mathcal{F}_0$-known initial states.
\end{itemize}
Define the two-gain modal controller (\lean{modalRead}) by
\[
  u_{r,k}^* = u_{\mathrm{com},k}^*(g_{\mathrm{com}})
            + u_{\mathrm{diff},r,k}^*(g_{\mathrm{diff}}),
  \qquad \sum_{r=1}^R u_{\mathrm{diff},r,k}^* = 0\text{ a.s.},
\]
where $u_{\mathrm{com}}^*$ applies \lean{lawRead} with gain $g_{\mathrm{com}}$
to the mean target $\bar S_k$ and $u_{\mathrm{diff},r}^*$ applies
\lean{lawRead} with gain $g_{\mathrm{diff}}$ to the deviation $S_{r,k} - \bar S_k$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Over every finite horizon $n$, $u^*$ is $\mathcal{F}$-adapted and
  minimizes expected $n$-cycle \lean{multiCycleCost} plus expected modal
  terminal value-to-go over all $\mathcal{F}$-adapted policies, and any
  minimizer equals $u^*$ almost surely on $k < n$.
\item Let $s$ be a constant. Suppose the two modes' floors sum to at least $s$
  at every cycle, and the revisions have bounded second moments. Then for every
  $\varepsilon > 0$, every $\mathcal{F}$-adapted policy's average expected cost
  exceeds $s - \varepsilon$ at infinitely many horizons
  (\lean{multi\_lipsync\_cost\_rate}). If the floors sum to at most $s$, the
  expected cost of $u^*$ over $n$ cycles is at most $n \cdot s$ plus its
  initial value-to-go (\lean{multi\_lipsync\_law\_cost\_le}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Centralized benchmark with unpriced latency ($\tilde c_\Theta = 0$)]
\label{thm:lowlat-opt}
In \path{ModalControl.lean} (\lean{multi\_lowlatency\_minimizes},
\lean{multi\_lowlatency\_unique\_ae}), under the same shared-filtration
hypotheses with $\tilde c_\Theta = 0$ and $\tilde c_S = \mathrm{weight}(g_{\mathrm{diff}})$,
$g_{\mathrm{diff}} \in (0, 1)$, the fixed-mean-pace controller
(\lean{consensusRead})
\[
  u_{r,k}^* = \bar u_{-1} + u_{\mathrm{diff},r,k}^*(g_{\mathrm{diff}}),
  \qquad \Delta \bar u_k^* = 0\text{ a.s.},
\]
minimizes the expected $n$-cycle \lean{multiCycleCost} (with $\tilde c_\Theta = 0$)
plus differential terminal value-to-go over all $\mathcal{F}$-adapted policies,
and any minimizer equals $u^*$ almost surely on $k < n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Mean latency drift and startup spread when $g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0$]
\label{rem:drift}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lowlat-opt} applies only to \lean{multiCycleCost} with
$\tilde c_\Theta = 0$, which contains neither the specification's linear latency
cost $c_\Lambda$ nor its missed-sample cost $c_M$. When $\bar u_k = \bar u_{-1}$
is held fixed forever, the group mean latency integrates the frequency
difference between the capture clock and the speakers' average clock. Even with
matched initial frequencies, a sender clock with random-walk frequency
coefficient $h_{-2} = 5\times 10^{-16}\,\text{Hz}$ (consumer quartz) accumulates
phase standard deviation $\sqrt{2\pi^2 h_{-2} T^3 / 3}$ over duration $T$:
about $12\,\text{ms}$ after one hour and $1.5\,\text{s}$ after one day,
eventually emptying or overflowing any fixed playout buffer. In addition, as
Section~\ref{sec:sim-sweeps} evaluates the modal gain trade-off in the
production protocol, and setting $g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0$ would prevent
receivers that initialize from different relayed measurements (due to relay
delay or packet loss) from ever reconciling their common-mode baselines,
leaving a permanent inter-speaker spread. Low-latency peer control therefore
retains a small positive common-mode gain ($g_{\mathrm{com,low}} > 0$) to
bound mean offset error and reconcile receiver baselines while keeping
common-mode roughness low.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Differential-mode disturbance split]
By the variance identity \lean{consensus\_one\_hot\_var}, if one speaker
$r_0$ incurs a step error $\delta_0$ while all other speakers stay aligned,
projecting the correction onto the zero-mean subspace reduces the immediate sum
of squared adjustments from $\delta_0^2$ to $\frac{R-1}{R}\delta_0^2$. However,
this projection leaves the group mean shifted by $\delta_0 / R$: in lip-sync
mode, removing that shift through the common mode adds
$R(\delta_0 / R)^2 = \delta_0^2 / R$ and restores the total $\delta_0^2$;
without a common-mode loop, the mean latency remains shifted by $\delta_0 / R$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Decentralized pure-star team control}

In a star topology where receivers do not exchange position errors, receiver
$r$ chooses $u_{r,k}$ using only its local filtration $(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})$.

\begin{theorem}[Decentralized star minimizer under uncorrelated errors]
\label{thm:star-weight}
In \path{ModalControl.lean} (\lean{decentralized\_star\_cost\_eq} and
\lean{decentralized\_star\_uncorrelated\_minimizes}), assume:
\begin{itemize}
\item $R > 0$, and $g_{\mathrm{indep}} \in (0, 1)$ satisfies
  $\mathrm{weight}(g_{\mathrm{indep}}) = \frac{R-1}{R}\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta$,
\item each receiver $r$ has its own filtration $(\mathcal{F}_{r,k})$ and
  satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:single-sep},
\item cross-receiver position errors $e_{r,k} = S_{r,k} - p_{r,k}$ are pairwise
  uncorrelated at every cycle $k < n$, $\E[e_{r,k} e_{r',k}] = 0$ for all
  $r \ne r'$, both under the candidate policy and under \lean{lawRead} with
  gain $g_{\mathrm{indep}}$.
\end{itemize}
Then running \lean{lawRead} with gain $g_{\mathrm{indep}}$ on each receiver's
local filtration minimizes expected $n$-cycle \lean{multiCycleCost} plus
averaged terminal value-to-go among all such uncorrelated decentralized
policies. The effective weight $\frac{R-1}{R}\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta$ is
larger than $\tilde c_\Theta$ (which ignores synchronization) and smaller than
$\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta$ because $1/R$ of a receiver's position error
moves the group mean.
\end{theorem}

Let $\phi_h(t)$ denote host $h$'s clock phase deviation ($C_h(t) = t + \phi_h(t)$)
and $\theta_r(t) = \phi_{\mathrm{src}}(t) - \phi_r(t)$ receiver $r$'s clock
offset relative to the sender $\mathrm{src}$. Whenever the sender's clock
wanders or the sender's transmit jitter is shared across links, the
uncorrelated-error hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:star-weight} fails: every
receiver tracks $\theta_r$, so the filtered sender-phase position error
$e_{\mathrm{com}}$ is shared across all $R$ receivers.

\begin{theorem}[Common-plus-private star cost identity]
\label{thm:star-split}
In \path{ModalControl.lean} (\lean{symmetric\_star\_cost\_split}), suppose each
receiver's position error and pace change decompose as
$e_r = e_{\mathrm{com}} + e_{\mathrm{priv},r}$ and
$\Delta u_r = \Delta u_{\mathrm{com}} + \Delta u_{\mathrm{priv},r}$, where
$e_{\mathrm{com}}$ is uncorrelated with each $e_{\mathrm{priv},r}$,
$\Delta u_{\mathrm{com}}$ is uncorrelated with each $\Delta u_{\mathrm{priv},r}$,
and the private errors $e_{\mathrm{priv},r}$ are pairwise uncorrelated across
$r \ne r'$. Then
\[
  \E[\mathrm{cost}_k]
  = \Bigl(\tilde c_\Theta\,\E[e_{\mathrm{com}}^2] + \E[\Delta u_{\mathrm{com}}^2]\Bigr)
  + \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R
    \Bigl(\bigl(\tfrac{R-1}{R}\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta\bigr)\E[e_{\mathrm{priv},r}^2]
      + \E[\Delta u_{\mathrm{priv},r}^2]\Bigr) .
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:star-split} is a cost identity, not a minimization. When
every receiver observes its true offset $\theta_r$ and frequency without
measurement noise, solving the discrete Lyapunov equation numerically yields a
shared gain $g_{\mathrm{star}}^*$ between
$\mathrm{gainForWeight}(\tilde c_\Theta)$ and
$\mathrm{gainForWeight}(\frac{R-1}{R}\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta)$.
However, \emph{even that numerical interval fails once measurement noise and a
Kalman filter are in the loop} (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}): a wandering
sender raises each receiver's Kalman gain, which passes more private measurement
noise into the regulator (charged at $\frac{R-1}{R}\tilde c_S + \tilde c_\Theta$)
and drives the best shared gain strictly \emph{below}
$\mathrm{gainForWeight}(\tilde c_\Theta)$. Moreover, a star receiver measures
only the difference $\theta_r = \phi_{\mathrm{src}} - \phi_r$ and cannot
separate sender wander from receiver wander from its own data.
Section~\ref{sec:sim-oracle} solves the coupled filter-in-the-loop Lyapunov
equation to quantify this effect.

\subsection{Companion spectral and state-space identities}

In \path{lean/Unisono/Analysis/Spectral.lean}, we record the algebraic
frequency-domain and steady-state filter identities companion to
Section~\ref{sec:control}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Discrete control factorization} (\lean{weight\_factor},
  \lean{step\_step}): on the unit circle $z = e^{i\omega}$,
  $\mathrm{weight}(g) + |1 - z|^4 = |P(z)|^2 / (1 - g)$ with
  $P(z) = z^2 - \frac{4(1-g)}{2-g} z + (1 - g)$, the characteristic polynomial
  of \lean{Regulation.step}.
\item \emph{Continuous-time Butterworth prototype} (\lean{butterworth}):
  $\beta_0^2 + \beta_2^2\omega^4 = |\beta_2(i\omega)^2 + \beta_1(i\omega) + \beta_0|^2$
  when $\beta_1^2 = 2 \beta_0 \beta_2$, giving a second-order Butterworth filter
  with damping ratio $\zeta = 1/\sqrt{2}$ (distinct from critical damping
  $\zeta = 1$), matching \path{docs/model.md}.
\item \emph{Scalar random-walk Riccati algebra} (\lean{filterRoot\_eq},
  \lean{filterRoot\_unique}, \lean{estimate\_factor}, \lean{riccati\_fixed},
  \lean{posterior\_variance}, \lean{revision\_variance}): for process-to-measurement
  variance ratio $\gamma = q / \sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}^2 > 0$, \lean{filterRoot}
  $\gamma$ is the unique root $\kappa \in (0, 1)$ of
  $\kappa^2 - (2 + \gamma)\kappa + 1 = 0$, giving the steady-state gain
  $K = 1 - \kappa$, algebraic Riccati fixed point
  $P_{\infty} = \sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}^2(1 - \kappa)/\kappa$, posterior identity
  $(1 - K)P_{\infty} = \sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}^2(1 - \kappa)$, innovation
  variance $P_{\infty} + \sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}^2$, and steady-state estimate
  revision variance $K^2(P_{\infty} + \sigma_{\mathrm{meas}}^2) = q$.
\item \emph{Two-state oscillator covariance matrix}
  (\lean{clockProcessCov\_nonneg}): for IEEE~Std~1139-2008 spectral parameters
  \cite{ieee1139} $q_0 = h_0/2$ and $q_2 = 2\pi^2 h_{-2}$ and any
  $\Delta t \ge 0$, the matrix
  \[
    Q(\Delta t) =
    \begin{pmatrix}
      q_0 \Delta t + \frac{1}{3} q_2 \Delta t^3 & \frac{1}{2} q_2 \Delta t^2 \\
      \frac{1}{2} q_2 \Delta t^2 & q_2 \Delta t
    \end{pmatrix}
  \]
  is positive semidefinite. (This proves matrix semidefiniteness; it does not
  construct a realization of \lean{SecondOrderMoments}.)
\end{itemize}

\section{Clock-offset estimation and star-versus-peer separation}
\label{sec:estimation}

Under separation (Section~\ref{sec:control}), synchronization accuracy depends
on estimating the relative clock offsets
$\theta_r(t) = \phi_{\mathrm{src}}(t) - \phi_r(t)$ and pairwise differences
$\phi_{r_2}(t) - \phi_{r_1}(t)$. Network queueing jitter and one-way delay
asymmetries enter as measurement error $\eta_r$ on the probes rather than as
part of $\theta_r$.

\subsection{Star filtering and sender-disciplined schedules}

Suppose receiver $r$ observes $\theta_r + \eta_r$ and applies a causal linear
filter $F_r$. In \path{lean/Unisono/Analysis/Estimation.lean}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Same vs.\ different filters}: if $F_1 = F_2 = F$, then
  $\phi_{\mathrm{src}}$ cancels from the pairwise estimation error between
  receivers $1$ and $2$ (\lean{star\_pair\_error\_same}). If $F_1 \ne F_2$
  (e.g.\ a fast filter on Ethernet and a slow filter on Wi-Fi), the pairwise
  error contains the leakage term $(F_1 - F_2)\phi_{\mathrm{src}}$
  (\lean{star\_pair\_error\_heterogeneous}).
\item \emph{Schedule substitution identity}
  (\lean{disciplined\_schedule\_error}): if the sender applies a schedule
  correction $s_{\mathrm{ref}}$ so that both receivers observe the shifted
  reference $\delta_{\mathrm{src}} = \phi_{\mathrm{src}} - s_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
  the same algebraic identity gives leakage $(F_1 - F_2)\delta_{\mathrm{src}}$.
  This lemma assumes identical, zero-delay delivery of $s_{\mathrm{ref}}$ to
  both receivers. Moreover, when $s_{\mathrm{ref}}$ estimates the sender's
  phase relative to the receivers' mean, $\delta_{\mathrm{src}}$ contains the
  precision-weighted mean of the receivers' phases plus estimation noise, which
  still leaks through $F_1 - F_2$ and shifts the group toward the receivers'
  mean clock rather than the capture clock. (In the production protocol,
  receivers instead run local star Kalman filters or peer Graph-Laplacian
  BLUE without sender-broadcast schedule feedback.)
\item \emph{Residual second moment} (\lean{residual\_variance\_sub},
  \lean{residual\_variance\_L2}): for zero-mean, pairwise uncorrelated errors
  $\eta_1, \dots, \eta_R$ with second moments $\sigma_r^2 > 0$,
  $V_{\mathrm{bc}} = (\sum_i \sigma_i^{-2})^{-1}$, weights
  $\omega_r = V_{\mathrm{bc}} / \sigma_r^2$, and weighted average
  $\bar\eta = \sum_i \omega_i \eta_i$,
  \[
    \E[\bar\eta^2] = V_{\mathrm{bc}},
    \qquad
    \E[(\eta_r - \bar\eta)^2] = \sigma_r^2 - V_{\mathrm{bc}} < \sigma_r^2 .
  \]
  The minus sign reflects the positive cross-moment
  $\E[\eta_r \bar\eta] = V_{\mathrm{bc}}$. Because $\E[\eta_i \eta_j] = 0$ for
  $i \ne j$ is required, this identity does not hold for biased errors such as
  queueing delay or link asymmetry.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Two-sided star-versus-peer asymmetry separation on \lean{Unisono.Spec}}

A round trip measures the sum of the two one-way delays, not their difference.
The transformation \lean{World.shiftHosts} in \path{Topologies.lean} shifts
host $h$'s timeline by $\Delta(h)$, with $\Delta(\mathrm{src}) = 0$. The delay
from $i$ to $j$ changes by $\Delta(j) - \Delta(i)$, and round-trip times do not
change (\lean{World.shiftHosts\_rtt\_eq}).

\lean{Solution.plays\_shiftHosts} shows that shifting host timelines changes no
playback decision. It assumes that shifted delays stay positive and that
event times are unbounded in both worlds. It assumes nothing about the order
of events at different hosts: the run handles messages that arrive at one
host at the same true time in a fixed order of the hosts that sent them,
which a shift does not change. This yields general lower bounds for
\emph{every} topology—both star and peer—when all links are shifted
(\lean{sync\_shiftHosts\_lower\_bound} and
\lean{tracking\_shiftHosts\_lower\_bound}). Any synchronization advantage of
peer communication therefore rests on direct receiver--receiver links remaining
symmetric while sender links carry unknown asymmetry.

To formalize this contrast directly on \lean{Unisono.Spec}, the limits module
\path{Spec/Limits.lean} defines \lean{shiftStarKeepPeers} $w\,\Delta$, which
applies $\Delta$ to clocks, speakers, and star links
($i = \mathrm{src} \lor j = \mathrm{src}$) while keeping all direct peer links
($i \ne \mathrm{src} \land j \ne \mathrm{src}$) unshifted.

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided star-versus-peer asymmetry separation on \lean{Unisono.Spec}]
\label{thm:star-peer-sep}
In \path{lean/Unisono/Spec/Limits.lean}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Invariance of star runs to peer links}
  (\lean{star\_states\_eq\_of\_star\_links\_eq}): modifying non-star links
  leaves the run states and playback choices of every star solution
  (\lean{Solution.IsStar}) unchanged.
\item \textbf{Star synchronization lower bound}
  (\lean{star\_sync\_lower\_bound\_with\_symmetric\_peers}): let
  $w^+ = \mathrm{shiftStarKeepPeers}\;w\,\Delta$ and
  $w^- = \mathrm{shiftStarKeepPeers}\;w\,(-\Delta)$ with $\Delta(\mathrm{src}) = 0$.
  Assume:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item on every star link ($i = \mathrm{src} \lor j = \mathrm{src}$), every
    delivered message in $w$ has delay strictly greater than
    $|\Delta(j) - \Delta(i)|$ (no delay bound is imposed on peer links),
  \item $\pi$ is a star solution (\lean{IsStar}) with unbounded event times
    (non-Zeno progress) in $w$, $w^+$, and $w^-$,
  \item capture times in $w$ are unbounded, and there exists $N_0 \in \mathbb{N}$
    such that every speaker plays every frame $n \ge N_0$ in $w$ (note: this
    hypothesis excludes worlds that permanently drop audio frames).
  \end{itemize}
  Then
  \[
    \max\bigl(w^+.\mathrm{sync}(\pi),\; w^-.\mathrm{sync}(\pi)\bigr)
    \ge \operatorname{Var}_r\bigl(\Delta(\mathrm{host}(r))\bigr) .
  \]
  An analogous lower bound $\ge A^2$ holds on \lean{World.tracking} for every
  topology under uniform shifts $\pm A$ (\lean{tracking\_shiftHosts\_lower\_bound}).
\item \textbf{Symmetric peer clock-reading identity}
  (\lean{peer\_probe\_recovers\_exact\_offset}): for two rate-1 clocks with
  constant offsets $b_1, b_2$ and constant symmetric link delay between
  non-sender hosts $h_1, h_2$, the NTP offset formula \cite{mills2010}
  $t_2 - \frac{t_1 + t_4}{2}$ equals the shifted offset difference
  $(b_2 - \Delta(h_2)) - (b_1 - \Delta(h_1))$.
\item \textbf{Two-sided separation and tight witness:}
  for any $A \in (0, 1/4)$, let $w^+ = \mathrm{sepWorldPlus}\;A$ and
  $w^- = \mathrm{sepWorldMinus}\;A$ be the pair of 3-host worlds with ideal
  rate-1 clocks, constant star-link delay $1/2$, constant symmetric peer-link
  delay $1/10$ (allowing $1/10 < 2A$, e.g.\ $A = 1/5$), speaker~1's request
  grid staggered by $1/3\,\text{s}$ from speaker~0's, and shifts
  $\Delta = (0, A, -A)$
  (\lean{peer\_beats\_star\_asymmetry\_bound}). Then:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item every qualifying star solution $\pi$ (meeting part~2) incurs
    $\max\bigl(w^+.\mathrm{sync}(\pi), w^-.\mathrm{sync}(\pi)\bigr) \ge A^2$;
  \item the bound is tight: the forwarding-only star witness
    \lean{ForwardStar.solution} satisfies \lean{IsStar} and progress, plays
    every frame $n \ge 2$,
    and attains $w^+.\mathrm{sync} = A^2$ and $w^-.\mathrm{sync} = A^2$
    (\lean{ForwardStar.sync\_eq});
  \item the idealized-world peer witness \lean{PeerSync.solution} uses only
    star links and the symmetric peer link $1 \leftrightarrow 2$
    (\lean{PeerSync.usesGraph}), makes unbounded progress
    (\lean{PeerSync.progress}), plays every frame $n \ge 2$ on both speakers,
    and achieves $w^+.\mathrm{sync} = 0$ and $w^-.\mathrm{sync} = 0$
    (\lean{PeerSync.sync\_eq\_zero}).
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\lean{PeerSync.solution} is a witness tailored to the idealized worlds
$(w^+, w^-)$, not a general protocol: it plays source position $k - 2$ (plus
its staggered offset and one peer-probe adjustment) without reading device
timestamps, relies on exact rate-1 clocks, one frame per block, constant
symmetric peer delay, and zero packet loss, drifts without bound if clock rates
differ, stalls speaker~1 if its single probe is lost, and incurs higher
tracking cost in $w^+$ ($(2 + A)^2$) than \lean{ForwardStar.solution}
($4 + A^2$).

\subsection{Graph-Laplacian BLUE, equal probe budgets, and asymmetry bias}

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a directed graph on $N_{\mathrm{host}}$ hosts where each
edge $a = (\mathrm{src}(a) \to \mathrm{dst}(a)) \in E$ provides a pairwise
offset measurement $y_a = \phi(\mathrm{dst}(a)) - \phi(\mathrm{src}(a)) + \eta_a$
with edge variance parameter $\sigma_a^2 > 0$ and static edge bias
$\alpha_a = \E[\eta_a]$. In \path{lean/Unisono/Analysis/Estimation.lean},
\lean{flowVariance} is defined algebraically as $\sum_{a \in E} \sigma_a^2 f_a^2$;
when edge errors are zero-mean and mutually independent with variances
$\sigma_a^2$, this equals the variance of the linear estimator
$\hat\theta_f = \sum_{a \in E} f_a y_a$. (Independence can fail when multiple
links share one Wi-Fi medium.)

\begin{theorem}[Kirchhoff unbiasedness, Thomson's principle, and edge bias]
\label{thm:thomson}
In \path{Estimation.lean}, for any two vertices $i, j \in V$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item (\lean{linear\_unbiased\_iff\_unitFlow}) When $\alpha_a = 0$ for all
  $a \in E$, $\hat\theta_f = \sum_{a \in E} f_a y_a$ recovers
  $\phi(j) - \phi(i)$ for all clock vectors $\phi$ if and only if $f$ is a unit
  $i \to j$ flow: $\operatorname{div}_G(f) = \mathbf{1}_j - \mathbf{1}_i$.
\item (\lean{thomson\_blue\_least\_variance}, \cite{doyle1984,barooah2007,giridhar2006})
  Let $\psi : V \to \R$ solve $L_G \psi = \mathbf{1}_j - \mathbf{1}_i$ where
  $B$ is the edge-vertex incidence matrix,
  $W = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_a^{-2})$, and $L_G = B^\top W B$, and set
  $f_a^* = (\psi(\mathrm{dst}(a)) - \psi(\mathrm{src}(a))) / \sigma_a^2$. Then
  every unit $i \to j$ flow $f$ satisfies
  \[
    \sum_{a \in E} \sigma_a^2 f_a^2
    = \underbrace{\bigl(\psi(j) - \psi(i)\bigr)}_{R_{\mathrm{eff}}(i, j)}
      + \sum_{a \in E} \sigma_a^2 (f_a - f_a^*)^2
    \ge R_{\mathrm{eff}}(i, j),
  \]
  with equality if and only if $f = f^*$.
\item (\lean{graph\_estimator\_bias}) Under nonzero edge biases $\alpha_a$, the
  bias of any unit $i \to j$ flow $f$ is $\sum_{a \in E} f_a \alpha_a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Solving the $(N_{\mathrm{host}}{-}1)\times(N_{\mathrm{host}}{-}1)$ symmetric
positive definite (SPD) reduced Laplacian system
$L_{\mathrm{red}} \hat\phi = B_{\mathrm{red}}^\top W y$
(pinning $\hat\phi_{\mathrm{src}} = 0$) yields the BLUE for every host offset
simultaneously, with covariance $L_{\mathrm{red}}^{-1}$ across the $R$
receivers. In particular, the variance of the group mean estimate
$\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R \hat\phi_r$ is
$\frac{1}{R^2}\mathbf{1}^\top L_{\mathrm{red}}^{-1}\mathbf{1}$ (not
$\frac{1}{R^2}\operatorname{tr}(L_{\mathrm{red}}^{-1})$, which ignores positive
off-diagonal correlations).

\begin{corollary}[Topology resistances, probe-budget scaling, and triangle bias]
\label{cor:topologies}
From Theorem~\ref{thm:thomson} (\path{Estimation.lean}), for $R \ge 2$ speakers
(so a speaker pair exists):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Star graph} (\lean{star\_effective\_resistance}): for speakers
  $r_1 \ne r_2$ with sender-link variances $\sigma_{r_1}^2, \sigma_{r_2}^2$,
  $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_1, r_2) = \sigma_{r_1}^2 + \sigma_{r_2}^2$.
\item \emph{Complete peer graph $K_{R+1}$ at equal per-link rate}
  (\lean{complete\_graph\_effective\_resistance}): on the complete graph
  $K_{R+1}$ (the sender plus $R$ speakers, $R(R+1)/2$ edges), if every edge has
  variance $\sigma^2$, $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_1, r_2) = \frac{2\sigma^2}{R+1}$, an
  $(R+1)\times$ variance reduction over the star ($2\sigma^2$).
\item \emph{Complete peer graph $K_{R+1}$ at equal total probe budget}
  (\lean{complete\_graph\_equal\_budget}): splitting the star's total probe
  rate ($R$ links at variance $\sigma^2$) uniformly across all $R(R+1)/2$ edges
  scales each edge's variance to $\sigma_K^2 = \frac{R+1}{2}\sigma^2$, yielding
  $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_1, r_2) = \sigma^2$—a $2\times$ reduction over the star's
  $2\sigma^2$ for every $R \ge 2$.
\item \emph{Wi-Fi sender plus wired peer link}
  (\lean{wifi\_sender\_wired\_peers\_resistance}): for two speakers sharing a
  Wi-Fi sender (variance $\sigma_W^2$) and connected by a wired peer link
  (variance $\sigma_P^2$),
  $R_{\mathrm{eff}}(1, 2) = \frac{2 \sigma_W^2 \sigma_P^2}{2 \sigma_W^2 + \sigma_P^2}
   < \min(\sigma_P^2, 2 \sigma_W^2)$.
\item \emph{Bias of the triangle BLUE vs.\ peer-only estimator}
  (\lean{triangle\_blue\_bias}): on the same triangle with sender-link biases
  $\alpha_0, \alpha_1$ and peer-link bias $\alpha_P$, the graph BLUE routes
  weight $\frac{\sigma_P^2}{2 \sigma_W^2 + \sigma_P^2}$ through the sender
  links, so its bias for $\phi_2 - \phi_1$ is
  \[
    \mathrm{bias}_{\mathrm{BLUE}}
    = \frac{\sigma_P^2}{2 \sigma_W^2 + \sigma_P^2}(\alpha_1 - \alpha_0)
    + \frac{2 \sigma_W^2}{2 \sigma_W^2 + \sigma_P^2}\alpha_P .
  \]
  With $\alpha_0 = +750\,\mu\text{s}$, $\alpha_1 = -750\,\mu\text{s}$, and
  $\alpha_P = 0$ (star bias $1500\,\mu\text{s}$): equal link standard deviations
  give $\mathrm{bias}_{\mathrm{BLUE}} = 500\,\mu\text{s}$; wired sender links
  ($5\,\mu\text{s}$) with a Wi-Fi peer link ($150\,\mu\text{s}$) give
  $1497\,\mu\text{s}$; and Wi-Fi sender links ($150\,\mu\text{s}$) with a wired
  peer link ($5\,\mu\text{s}$) give $0.83\,\mu\text{s}$. Zero inter-speaker
  bias holds only for the peer-only estimator $f_{\mathrm{peer}} = (0, 0, 1)$,
  whose bias is $\alpha_P$.
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}

\section{Playout latency margin in a standalone Campbell pause model}
\label{sec:level}

To choose the nominal playout safety margin $m \ge 0$ (seconds above baseline
path and processing delay), \path{Analysis/Level.lean} analyzes a
standalone continuous-time Campbell-formula model \cite{kingman1993}.
This module imports only Mathlib and is not tied to
\lean{Unisono.Spec.Objectives}. Several modeling differences separate it from
the discrete specification:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pauses vs.\ outages.} A link \emph{pause} of duration $d$ delays
  packets by $d$ seconds before delivering them, leaving $(d - m)^+$ seconds of
  empty buffer and one discontinuity whenever $d > m$. During an \emph{outage}
  that drops packets, the entire episode is missed regardless of $m$; the model
  below describes pauses only.
\item \emph{Unit conversion.} The model counts missed audio in seconds per
  second, whereas \lean{World.missed} counts missed output samples per second,
  so the missed-seconds weight is $\tilde c_M = \rho c_M$.
\item \emph{Discrete effects omitted.} Device block-request boundaries,
  resampler lookahead, background jitter tails, and overlapping pauses are not
  modeled in $J_{\mathrm{buf}}(m)$ (they are evaluated in simulation in
  Sections~\ref{sec:sim-oracle} and~\ref{sec:sim-topologies}).
\end{itemize}

Let $\nu = \lambda\mu$ be a finite pause intensity measure (arrival rate
$\lambda$ times duration distribution $\mu$), and let $m_0 \ge 0$ be the target
lip-sync margin (target latency minus baseline path and processing delay). The
steady-state per-second margin cost is
\[
  J_{\mathrm{buf}}(m) = c_\Lambda m + c_\Theta (m - m_0)^2
       + \tilde c_M \int (d - m)^+\,d\nu(d)
       + c_D\,\nu\{d > m\} .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Playout margin minimizers in the Campbell pause model]
\label{thm:level}
In \path{lean/Unisono/Analysis/Level.lean}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Linearity over averaged pause measures}
  (\lean{fleet\_average\_missed\_cost}): for $R > 0$ finite pause measures
  $\nu_1, \dots, \nu_R$ with finite first moments, averaging
  $\int (d - m)^+\,d\nu_r(d)$ across $r$ equals the integral against the
  averaged measure $\bar\nu = \frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^R \nu_r$. (This is integral
  linearity for the Campbell model, not a theorem about \lean{World.missed}.)
\item \emph{Quantile rule without discontinuity pricing} ($c_D = 0$):
  when $c_\Theta \ge 0$, $\tilde c_M \ge 0$, and $\nu$ has finite first moment,
  any $m \in \R$ satisfying
  \[
    \tilde c_M\,\nu\{d > m\} \le c_\Lambda + 2 c_\Theta (m - m_0)
    \le \tilde c_M\,\nu\{d \ge m\}
  \]
  globally minimizes $J_{\mathrm{buf}}(m)$ over $\R$ (\lean{margin\_best\_missed},
  \lean{margin\_best\_tracking}).
\item \emph{Closed-form exponential minimizer of $J_{\mathrm{buf}}$ with discontinuities in low-latency
  mode} (\lean{margin\_best\_exponential\_with\_clicks}): assume
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $c_\Theta = 0$, $c_\Lambda > 0$, $\tilde c_M \ge 0$, $c_D \ge 0$,
  \item pauses arrive at rate $\lambda > 0$ with a single shared exponential
    duration distribution of mean $\bar d > 0$ (a mixture of exponentials with
    different means across speakers is not exponential).
  \end{itemize}
  Then $J_{\mathrm{buf}}(m) = c_\Lambda m + \lambda(\tilde c_M \bar d + c_D) e^{-m / \bar d}$
  is globally minimized on $m \ge 0$ by
  \[
    m^* = \max\!\left(0,\;
      \bar d \ln\!\left(\frac{\lambda\,(\tilde c_M + c_D / \bar d)}{c_\Lambda}\right)\right) .
  \]
\item \emph{Non-convexity under atomic pauses}
  (\lean{discontinuity\_nonconvex\_example}): for a fixed pause duration
  $d_0 > 0$ arriving at rate $\lambda > 0$, whenever $c_D \lambda > c_\Lambda d_0$
  the step indicator $\mathbf{1}_{m < d_0}$ creates a downward jump at $m = d_0$
  with $J_{\mathrm{buf}}(d_0) < J_{\mathrm{buf}}(0)$ and
  $J_{\mathrm{buf}}(d_0/2) > \frac{1}{2}(J_{\mathrm{buf}}(0) + J_{\mathrm{buf}}(d_0))$.
  General pause distributions with $c_D > 0$ therefore require a 1D search over
  candidate margins rather than a local quantile rule \cite{unisono-spec}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Production core proofs and controller design summary}
\label{sec:impl}

The fixed-point real-time production core in \path{crates/unisono-core}
implements the four-timestamp Network Time Protocol (NTP) minimum-RTT
selector (\path{min\_rtt.rs}), the Joseph-form two-state Kalman filter
(\path{kalman.rs}), the square-root-free lower-diagonal-transpose
($\mathrm{LDL}^\top$) Graph-Laplacian BLUE solver (\path{blue.rs}), the
unified peer/star synchronization engine (\path{sync.rs}), and the two-mode
bounded playback regulator (\lean{ModalPlayer} in \path{player.rs}). All core
modules are translated from Rust to Lean by Aeneas and cross-checked against
native Rust execution on concrete inputs (\texttt{just translation-check}).

In \path{lean/Unisono/Solutions/Core.lean}, we compose these Aeneas-translated
production core functions into a \lean{Unisono.Spec.Solution} (\lean{Core.solution})
using strictly local host observations. Because \path{unisono-runtime} is not
translated to Lean, \lean{Core.solution} is a Lean composition of the core
functions (without device-line fitting, independent timer events, or separate
$t_3$ send reads) rather than a proof about the runtime threads. The following
properties are proved and checked in \path{lean/Unisono/Audit.lean} under
Lean's standard axioms only:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Graph membership and exact Star degeneration}: every transmitted
  packet obeys the declared sender and static peer allowlist
  (\lean{usesGraph\_of\_peers}); when a host's peer list is empty, it
  satisfies \lean{Solution.IsStar} (\lean{isStar\_of\_no\_peers}), \lean{Sync}
  keeps zero peers (\lean{sync\_zero\_peers\_inv}), emits zero peer probes or
  relays (\lean{star\_no\_peer\_messages}), rejects incoming peer packets,
  exports a Star snapshot matching the sender Kalman filter
  (\lean{star\_sync\_snapshot\_eq\_sender\_kalman}), and
  \lean{ModalPlayer.\allowbreak cycle} matches single-mode
  \lean{Player.\allowbreak cycle} at \lean{star\_gain}
  (\lean{star\_modal\_player\_eq\_single\_mode}).
\item \emph{Non-Zeno progress and liveness under
  \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds}} (\lean{progress} and
  \lean{request\_step}): in every world satisfying
  \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds}, the run advances past every true time
  and reaches every speaker playback-block request.
\item \emph{Within-block speed and conditional boundary-step bounds}
  (\lean{modal\_cycle\_full\_spec}, \lean{receiver\_cycle\_plan\_spec},
  \lean{rcv\_modal\_inv}, \lean{withinBlock\_speed\_bound},
  \lean{boundary\_step\_safe}): \lean{Core.\allowbreak solution} preserves
  \lean{use\_sync = true} and regulation settings across all events; every
  consecutive sample pair inside a playback block unconditionally satisfies the
  \lean{World.\allowbreak speed} bound under
  \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds}; and across adjacent non-empty blocks
  without intervening restarts or 64-bit unsigned frame overflow (and with
  \lean{jump} $\ge 3$), every continuous boundary step satisfies
  $|y - x - 1| \le \mathrm{paceBound}$. Full unconditional
  \lean{Solution.\allowbreak WithinSpeed} across all boundaries remains open.
\item \emph{Component-level ideal-world exactness}: within the 64-bit
  nanosecond range on constant noiseless inputs, \lean{ntp\_exchange} recovers
  exact offset and RTT (\lean{ideal\_ntp\_exchange\_exact}), the Kalman
  zero-innovation state is an exact fixed point
  (\lean{ideal\_kalman\_and\_sender\_time\_exact}),
  \lean{ModalPlayer.\allowbreak cycle} plans unity step
  (\lean{ideal\_modal\_cycle\_unity}), and any trajectory already on the ideal
  unity-step schedule at the target latency attains zero synchronization,
  tracking, roughness, missed-sample, and discontinuity costs
  (\lean{ideal\_zero\_cost\_objectives}, which takes that trajectory as a
  hypothesis and does not mention \lean{Core.\allowbreak solution}).
\end{itemize}
No theorem in \path{Solutions/Core.lean} currently connects a run of
\lean{Core.\allowbreak solution} to any of the six
\lean{Unisono.\allowbreak Spec.\allowbreak Objectives} under any approved
model—whether \lean{IdealConstantWorld}, \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds},
\lean{StationaryNetworkDisturbances}, \lean{SecondOrderMoments}, or
\lean{StochasticWorld}. Empirical performance of the shipped runtime is
measured by the black-box PCM simulator in Section~\ref{sec:sim}.

\paragraph{Controller design summary.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Deduplicated sliding-window minimum-RTT probe selector.} Retain the
  last $N_{\mathrm{win}} = 8$ received replies without blocking on dropped User
  Datagram Protocol (UDP) packets, deduplicate by monotonic exchange identifier,
  inflate initial single-sample variance to $(\mathrm{rtt}/2)^2$ so a queued
  first exchange cannot pull the frequency estimate to its clamp, estimate
  steady-state measurement variance online from the median excess RTT above the
  window minimum floored by a configurable variance floor (default
  $(5\,\mu\text{s})^2$), and gate relaying the sender edge $(0, \text{self})$
  until four exchanges have populated the window.
\item \emph{Timestamped fixed-point Kalman filter in Joseph form.} Store
  posterior $(\hat\theta, \hat f, P)$ at midpoint timestamp $t_{\mathrm{last}}$,
  predict across $\Delta t \ge 0$ in sub-steps of at most $1000\,\text{s}$
  (advancing $t_{\mathrm{last}}$ across arbitrarily long silences without lag)
  using integer-scaled $Q(\Delta t)$ from the configured \texttt{--clock-prior},
  update $P$ in Joseph's stabilized form \cite{bucy1968} with explicit
  positive-semidefiniteness clamping, and export monotonic fixed-point
  \lean{Line} snapshots to the audio thread.
\item \emph{Decentralized Graph-Laplacian BLUE with \texttt{--link-weight}.}
  Each receiver exchanges direct \lean{PeerProbe}/\lean{PeerEcho} probes with
  its static peer allowlist, tags \lean{PeerRelay} frames with
  \texttt{sender\_clock\_id} (fencing stale relays across sender reboots) and
  actual measurement age \texttt{age\_ns} (with symmetric variance growth to
  relay send time), estimates per-edge slopes using a dedicated $500\,\text{ms}$
  anchor spacing so relays at $250\,\text{ms}$ cadence update slopes reliably,
  ages out stale direct sender edges in peer mode after $5\,\text{s}$, applies
  optional per-link precision weights (\texttt{--link-weight}) once at graph
  assembly, and solves the reduced
  $(N_{\mathrm{host}}{-}1)\times(N_{\mathrm{host}}{-}1)$ Laplacian via
  fixed-point $\mathrm{LDL}^\top$. Peer-mode entry preserves the running
  sender frequency estimate when re-anchoring the common and differential
  filters.
\item \emph{Bumpless two-mode playback regulation (\lean{ModalPlayer}).}
  In Star mode (\lean{peer\_active = false}), \lean{ModalPlayer} runs
  single-mode \lean{Regulation.law} at \texttt{--star-gain}. In Peer mode, it
  splits the current pace bumplessly on transition and regulates common and
  differential modes separately at \texttt{--common-gain} and
  \texttt{--diff-gain} (default $(0.5\,\text{s}^{-1}, 4.0\,\text{s}^{-1})$ for
  lip-sync and $(0.05\,\text{s}^{-1}, 4.0\,\text{s}^{-1})$ for low-latency,
  avoiding the startup spread and mean-latency drift of $g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0$).
\end{enumerate}

noise $h_{-2}$, and 8-pole Ornstein--Uhlenbeck $1/f$ flicker frequency noise

and heterogeneous post-$t_3$ kernel/NIC send latency; links model asymmetric
baselines, bimodal queueing, packet loss, reordering, pauses, and burst
outages; and shared access-point topologies add correlated per-direction medium
contention (\texttt{SharedContentionModel}). Every production run lasts
$301.5\,\text{s}$ and scores the true steady-state window
$[200\,\text{s}, 299\,\text{s})$ across $10$ deterministic seeds, reporting
medians and interquartile ranges (IQR, $[p_{25}, p_{75}]$) on every metric plus
maximum sync RMS across seeds. Throughout this section, RMS denotes
root-mean-square, synchronization RMS is $\sqrt{S}$ in microseconds, peak
spread is the maximum absolute pairwise playout difference, tracking RMS is
$\sqrt{\Theta}$, latency is mean latency $\Lambda$, roughness is $R$ in
$\text{s}^{-2}$, missed is missed output samples per second, and clicks is
discontinuities per second.

Table~\ref{tab:topo} highlights five empirical steady-state findings on the
shipped pipelines:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Ideal baseline, calm wired LANs, and uncalibrated DAC sensitivity.}
  On ideal constant links, Peer attains $0.00\,\mu\text{s}$ sync RMS and
  tracking error ($0.0\,\mu\text{s}$ peak, $5.93{\times}10^{-8}\,\text{s}^{-2}$
  black-box 24-bit PCM floor roughness, zero misses). On calm wired Ethernet
  with realistic device timestamp jitter and heterogeneous post-$t_3$ send
  latencies, Star ($1.35\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR $[1.11, 1.43]\,\mu\text{s}$), Peer
  lip-sync ($1.92\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR $[1.40, 2.13]\,\mu\text{s}$), Peer
  low-latency ($1.94\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR $[1.43, 2.15]\,\mu\text{s}$), and
  co-located 3-host/4-speaker runs ($1.41$ Star, $1.83\,\mu\text{s}$ Peer) all
  stay within $2\,\mu\text{s}$ RMS with zero missed samples or clicks, whereas
  legacy Hull degrades to $15.70\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR $[15.14, 17.80]\,\mu\text{s}$,
  peak $102.7\,\mu\text{s}$) under realistic device and timestamp noise. Star
  tracks slightly tighter than Peer on symmetric calm wired links because
  relayed edges carry finite relay age and heterogeneous peer post-$t_3$ send
  latencies; adding $+100\,\mu\text{s}$ of uncalibrated DAC latency to one
  speaker shifts sync RMS directly to $49.75\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR
  $[49.54, 49.99]\,\mu\text{s}$, peak $107.2\,\mu\text{s}$).
\item \emph{Wireless wandering sender to wired peers (default prior).} Even
  with the mismatched CLI default \texttt{--clock-prior consumer} on a
  wandering Wi-Fi sender, Peer lip-sync routes differential estimation across
  the wired peer mesh and reduces steady-state sync RMS by $18.3\times$ over
  Star: from $40.06\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR $[39.18, 47.38]\,\mu\text{s}$, peak
  $160.8\,\mu\text{s}$) down to $2.19\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR
  $[2.00, 2.28]\,\mu\text{s}$, max $2.6\,\mu\text{s}$, peak $10.0\,\mu\text{s}$),
  and $66\times$ over legacy Hull ($144.41\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR
  $[133.48, 154.79]\,\mu\text{s}$). Shipped retransmission repairs the $2\%$
  random Wi-Fi packet drops on all four policies, leaving only rare pauses
  exceeding $90\,\text{ms}$ ($3.03$--$3.07\,\text{samples/s}$, IQR
  $[1.64, 12.47]\,\text{s}^{-1}$, $0.005\,\text{clicks/s}$).
\item \emph{Shared access-point contention.} With two wired and two Wi-Fi
  receivers sharing a contended wireless access point, Peer reduces steady-state
  sync RMS by $3.5\times$ ($34.97$ [IQR $32.04, 38.15$] $\to 10.02$ [IQR
  $7.72, 17.59$]\,$\mu\text{s}$), peak spread by $4.1\times$ ($154.6$ [IQR
  $143.5, 161.9$] $\to 37.4$ [IQR $30.2, 60.4$]\,$\mu\text{s}$), and tracking
  error by $2.7\times$ ($43.4$ [IQR $37.9, 49.7$] $\to 16.2$ [IQR
  $11.6, 19.6$]\,$\mu\text{s}$), while roughness and pause-induced misses stay
  comparable across both policies.
\item \emph{Lip-sync vs.\ low-latency modal trade-off.} Lowering
  $g_{\mathrm{com}}$ from $0.50\,\text{s}^{-1}$ to $0.05\,\text{s}^{-1}$ widens
  common-mode tracking error ($8.3$ [IQR $7.1, 8.9$] $\to 13.9$ [IQR
  $10.2, 15.2$]\,$\mu\text{s}$ wired; $278.0$ [IQR $242.9, 401.9$] $\to 373.1$
  [IQR $313.4, 500.2$]\,$\mu\text{s}$ Wi-Fi) while keeping sync RMS virtually
  unchanged ($1.92 \to 1.94\,\mu\text{s}$ wired; $2.19 \to 2.25\,\mu\text{s}$
  Wi-Fi).
\item \emph{Shipped retransmission and adaptive playout level.} On a 1-to-1
  bursty wireless link, enabling the exact shipped defaults
  (\texttt{kept = 256}, \texttt{Choice::shipped\_default} at
  $500\,\mu\text{s/s}$ slope, \texttt{ShippedAudio<256,64,8,64>}) cuts missed
  output samples by $99.5\%$ ($2267.43$ [IQR $2242.97, 2282.85$] $\to 10.91$
  [IQR $3.12, 31.99$]\,$\text{s}^{-1}$), discontinuities by $99.6\%$ ($5.717$
  [IQR $5.646, 5.841$] $\to 0.020$ [IQR $0.010, 0.020$]\,$\text{s}^{-1}$), and
  gap-induced roughness by four orders of magnitude ($52.7$ [IQR $50.0, 61.8$]
  $\to 9.68{\times}10^{-4}$ [IQR $6.08{\times}10^{-4}, 1.68{\times}10^{-2}$]\,$\text{s}^{-2}$),
  by adapting mean latency from $28.61$ (IQR $[28.59, 28.63]$) to $94.82\,\text{ms}$
  (IQR $[68.83, 114.47]\,\text{ms}$; tracking RMS $64.9\,\text{ms}$ reflects
  that adaptation against the fixed $30\,\text{ms}$ \lean{World.target}).
\end{enumerate}

Sweeps~A--D show four practical properties—and failure modes—of the shipped
implementation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Asymmetric sensitivity to \texttt{--clock-prior} mismatch (Sweep~A).}
  Overestimating oscillator noise (\texttt{consumer} or \texttt{wandering}) is
  safe and improves steady-state accuracy on both wired ($1.13$--$2.00\,\mu\text{s}$)
  and Wi-Fi-source topologies ($1.54$--$2.19\,\mu\text{s}$); matching
  \texttt{--sender-clock-prior wandering} cuts Wi-Fi tracking error by
  $2.6\times$ ($278.0$ [IQR $242.9, 401.9$] $\to 105.0$ [IQR
  $80.4, 148.1$]\,$\mu\text{s}$). By contrast, setting the \emph{local} prior
  too tight (\texttt{tcxo / tcxo}) on real quartz oscillators severely degrades
  sync RMS: $30.41\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR $[22.11, 49.49]\,\mu\text{s}$, max
  $73.7\,\mu\text{s}$) wired and $51.92\,\mu\text{s}$ (IQR
  $[28.86, 63.27]\,\mu\text{s}$, max $74.4\,\mu\text{s}$) on Wi-Fi$\to$3w.
  The default \texttt{consumer / consumer} preset avoids this failure while
  requiring no per-host oscillator knowledge.
\item \emph{Variance floor trade-off (Sweep~B).} In steady state, the default
  $5\,\mu\text{s}$ floor keeps both wired ($1.92\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR
  $[1.40, 2.13]\,\mu\text{s}$) and Wi-Fi-source peer sync ($2.21\,\mu\text{s}$,
  IQR $[1.69, 2.34]\,\mu\text{s}$) near $2\,\mu\text{s}$; raising the floor to
  $25\,\mu\text{s}$ unnecessarily floors the wired peer link itself and more
  than doubles sync RMS on both topologies ($4.36\,\mu\text{s}$ wired,
  $5.06\,\mu\text{s}$ Wi-Fi$\to$peer).
\item \emph{Unanchored $g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0$ fails in steady state (Sweep~C).}
  Setting $g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0.00\,\text{s}^{-1}$ leaves mean latency
  unanchored ($91.42\,\text{ms}$, IQR $[91.06, 92.92]\,\text{ms}$; tracking
  $2599.2\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR $[2310.0, 3084.6]\,\mu\text{s}$), doubles missed
  samples ($4.58$ vs.\ $2.27\,\text{s}^{-1}$), and leaves large inter-speaker
  spread across seeds: median $28.25\,\mu\text{s}$, IQR
  $[2.87, 129.13]\,\mu\text{s}$, max $167.5\,\mu\text{s}$ (peak
  $2509.6\,\mu\text{s}$). Enabling even a small positive common-mode gain
  ($g_{\mathrm{com}} = 0.05\,\text{s}^{-1}$ low-latency or
  $0.50\,\text{s}^{-1}$ lip-sync) collapses sync RMS to $2.09$--$2.25\,\mu\text{s}$
  (max $2.7\,\mu\text{s}$) and peak spread to $\approx 10\,\mu\text{s}$.
  Raising $g_{\mathrm{com}}$ further to $2.00\,\text{s}^{-1}$ yields negligible
  tracking benefit ($287.0 \to 278.2\,\mu\text{s}$) while raising roughness by
  $33\%$ ($5.16 \to 6.87{\times}10^{-4}\,\text{s}^{-2}$).
\item \emph{Two-sided asymmetry behavior of \texttt{--link-weight} (Sweep~D).}
  No node can observe which link is asymmetric, so Table~\ref{tab:asym} reports
  both sides. When asymmetry lies on the sender links ($200$, $1000$,
  $2000\,\mu\text{s}$), Star sits on the Theorem~\ref{thm:star-peer-sep} floor
  ($100.12$, $500.01$, $999.95\,\mu\text{s}$), unweighted Peer
  ($w_{\mathrm{src}} = 1.00$) sits on the $1/3$ triangle BLUE floor ($33.73$,
  $167.10$, $333.90\,\mu\text{s}$ vs.\ $33.3$, $166.7$, $333.3\,\mu\text{s}$),
  and downweighting the sender ($w_{\mathrm{src}} = 0.05$) cuts sync RMS by
  $30$--$41\times$ relative to Star ($3.38$, $12.42$, $24.24\,\mu\text{s}$ vs.\
  theoretical $2.4$, $12.2$, $24.4\,\mu\text{s}$). Conversely, when a
  $200$ or $1000\,\mu\text{s}$ one-way asymmetry lies on the \emph{peer} link
  instead, Star is immune ($1.35\,\mu\text{s}$), unweighted Peer degrades to
  $33.09$ and $166.31\,\mu\text{s}$ (theory $33.3$, $166.7\,\mu\text{s}$), and
  \texttt{--link-weight 0.05} \emph{amplifies} the error to $48.48$ and
  $243.69\,\mu\text{s}$ ($36$--$180\times$ worse than Star, matching theory
  $48.8$ and $243.9\,\mu\text{s}$). Downweighting the sender edge via
  \texttt{--link-weight} is therefore appropriate only when the operator knows
  the direct peer mesh is symmetric relative to asymmetric sender paths.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open gaps and future work}

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Objective bounds for \lean{Solutions.\allowbreak Core}.}
  The module \path{Solutions/Core.lean} proves graph membership, exact Star
  degeneration, non-Zeno progress and block-request liveness under
  \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds}, within-block speed bounds
  unconditionally under \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds} (plus the
  conditional adjacent-block bound \lean{boundary\_step\_safe}), and
  component-level ideal-world exactness. Connecting a run of
  \lean{Core.\allowbreak solution} to any of the six
  \lean{Unisono.\allowbreak Spec.\allowbreak Objectives} in any model
  (including \lean{IdealConstantWorld}, \lean{World.\allowbreak WithinBounds},
  and stochastic models), closing unconditional
  \lean{Solution.\allowbreak WithinSpeed} across silence restarts, and linking
  \path{unisono-runtime} to \lean{Core.\allowbreak solution} remain open.
\item \emph{Integer-to-real estimator equivalence (\texttt{MinRtt},
  \texttt{Kalman}, \texttt{Blue}).} \path{lean/Unisono/MinRtt.lean},
  \path{Kalman.lean}, and \path{Blue.lean} prove totality, exact single-exchange
  NTP arithmetic, variance floors, PSD preservation via explicit clamping, and
  single-edge Star recovery, but not exact minimum-RTT selection over the
  window, closeness of the fixed-point Kalman recursion to real-valued
  filtering, or finite-precision error bounds comparing integer
  $\mathrm{LDL}^\top$ against real-valued
  \lean{Analysis.\allowbreak Estimation.\allowbreak thomson\_blue\_least\_variance} on
  multi-peer graphs.
\item \emph{Nonzero clock noise in \lean{SecondOrderMoments}.} The witness
  \lean{SecondOrderMoments.\allowbreak example\_holds} currently gives zero
  noise to clocks, devices, and timestamps (with nonzero uncorrelated link
  jitter). Band-limiting ensures second-moment compatibility with bounded clock
  rates (\lean{secondDifferenceCovariance\_diag\_le}), but constructing a
  random strictly increasing \lean{Clock} with nonzero \lean{ClockNoise} and
  proving theorems from \lean{SecondOrderMoments} or \lean{StochasticWorld}
  remain open.
\item \emph{Online noise identification and correlated-medium weighting.}
  Estimating oscillator noise $(h_0, h_{-2})$ and pause parameters
  $(\lambda, \bar d)$ online within short startup windows, characterizing
  cost-minimizing decentralized control for general non-symmetric star and peer
  networks with delayed, lossy messages, and extending Graph-Laplacian BLUE to
  correlated shared-medium Wi-Fi jitter remain open problems.
\end{itemize}
